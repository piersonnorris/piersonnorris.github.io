Layout is deterministic — a hashed seed per note id, a golden-angle spiral around a per-domain sky anchor, then ~150 passes of collision relaxation in aspect-corrected units — so the constellation is the *same shape* on every load and still spreads properly on a phone (measured: 39px minimum gap at 390px wide, 48px at desktop). The moon is a no-fly zone. Arrow keys walk star to star geometrically; every star is focusable and titled.
- **Jars are the domains.** Six glass jars half-buried in the sand, one per top-level domain, each with a firefly per note (capped at 7) and a rope-tied paper tag carrying the label and count. Opening a jar lights that domain's stars, dims the rest to 9%, names them, and lets three fireflies drift out of the lid.
- **The telescope draws the links.** Press it and 30 constellation lines draw themselves in on a staggered `stroke-dashoffset`; press again to stow it. Selecting a star traces *its own* lines even with the telescope stowed, so you are never lost.
- **Pointing at a star does everything selecting it does, except open the panel** (Pierce, 2026-09-09: *"make the stars more interactive for users to use"*). Hover or keyboard-focus a star and it swells, its own lines light, its neighbours lift, the rest of the sky steps back to 38%, and a small **peek card** names it — domain, source, when it was learned, how many links, and "press to open the field log". The card is deliberately tiny (224×109) and goes to whichever side of the star has more empty sky, because a card thrown onto the crowded side covers the neighbours it just lit. Hover and selection share one code path — `state.hoverId || state.noteId` is the sky's single "what is this pointing at", so the two can never disagree.
  - **The links are objects too.** Each line has an invisible 15px twin underneath it, live only while the telescope is out: hover a line and both lessons it joins light up and say their names.
  - **The deck reaches the sky.** Hovering a title in the field notes, the ship's log, or a rope link in the open panel lights that star in the constellation — without a card, since the star may be scrolled out of view.
  - **Also:** a ring pulses off a pressed star, clicking empty sky puts the sky down (closes the panel), and a touch gets the highlight but never the card — a tap there is a press, and a card would be in the way.
  - **One bug found doing it:** hovering a title in the field notes and then moving to that same star showed no card. The id had not changed, so the "did it change" guard returned early and the card was never built. "Is there a card up" is now asked separately from "did the note change".
- **The field log.** Pressing a star opens a parchment-on-wood panel — crumb, handwritten title, learned date, evidence chips, the body with clickable `[[wikilinks]]` that fly you to that star, and rope-tag lists of lines out / lines in. On desktop the shore is *padded*, not covered: the panel takes 420px and the percentage-positioned sky reflows into what's left, so the panel never hides the constellation it describes. Bottom sheet under 1080px. Esc closes; `#note-id` still deep-links, and now a `hashchange` listener makes a pasted link work while already on the page.
- **Every line is still real.** The sky computes links itself (the page no longer loads `PNGraphify`), so a new test — `tools/tracker/atlas-links.test.js` — pins the two implementations together: identical undirected pairs, identical unresolved count, plus unique ids/titles, every note in a declared domain, and nothing adrift. Verified failing on a typo'd `[[wikilink]]` before being accepted. Shape unchanged from R16: **25 stars, 30 lines, 2.4 lines per star, 0 adrift, 0 unresolved**.
- **One stat was wrong and is now right.** R16's status bar divided links by notes and printed `1.2`, while this file has always said 2.4 per note. 2.4 is average *degree* — each undirected line counted at both ends — which is the number "lines per star" actually asks for. Fixed in the rebuild.

(2) `vector-effect:non-scaling-stroke` (needed to keep strokes even under a stretched viewBox) makes the browser compute `stroke-dasharray` in screen space, which broke the `pathLength="1"` draw-on and rendered every line dashed; the SVG now uses a 1:1 pixel viewBox kept in sync by a `ResizeObserver`.
