Additional eventsJun 9 – Aug 7, 2026

AI Intern, Screencastify

Screencastify · nine weeks

Rather than guessing where the bottlenecks were, I wrote one three-round workflow-mapping prompt and dropped it in a shared folder. Department leaders mapped their own work — trigger, middle, hand-offs, friction, root cause — and seven workflows across six departments came back in 48 hours, with exactly one meeting added to anyone's calendar.

All seven had the same failure: a person acting as the API between systems that don't talk. That isn't seven problems. It's one problem, seven times.

  • 7workflows
  • 6departments
  • 48hours
  • 3fixes shipped
The three fixes
  • Finance — weekly AR collectionsManual reconciliation across a billing system, an inbox, and a tracking sheet. Replaced with CSV in → prioritized outreach list, category and aging summary out. 4–8 hrs/week down to under 15 minutes.
  • Product marketing — Monday update emailA manual analytics pull, then writing the narrative from memory. Replaced with a saved prompt template: paste the export, get a formatted draft. ~30 min/week plus the rework loop, gone.
  • Engineering — sprint review recordingBlocked every week waiting on ticket updates. Fixed with a hard-cutoff policy and one saved filter — no tool build at all. Stalls became part of the story instead of a blocker.

None of the three needed engineering time. Combined build time: one afternoon.

The distribution audit
  • Audited 76 posts across three channels and found the problem wasn't effort — the cadence was scattered and nothing anchored it. The reframe: not under-working, under-concentrating.
  • Proposed the Content Waterfall — one ungated anchor asset systematically repurposed into eight placements (two short-form A/B cuts → the winner promoted and syndicated → long-form for search → a professional-network cut → a thread), plus a single named distribution owner instead of a committee.

The method is the résumé line — find the problem in someone's actual Tuesday, get sign-off, build it with them, ship it.

Wrote the crew training program

True North Services LLC · head-manager service training

A field-standards deck written from scratch so a new manager can run a job without shadowing one first. Three services, each with a six-step procedure and its own do's and don'ts.

What's in it
  • Power washingInspect and prep, tip and pressure selection, test in a discreet spot, pre-treat, top-to-bottom overlapping passes, rinse and walkthrough — plus the failure modes that cost money: holding the spray in one place, narrow tips on soft material, spraying up under siding.
  • Paver sand reapplicationClear the joints, confirm the surface is dry, sweep diagonally, compact and top off, clear every grain off the paver faces, then mist to activate. Dry surface and cure time are non-negotiable.
  • Window washingDebris first, soak the pane, scrub side to side including the corners, overlapping squeegee strokes with the blade wiped each pass, microfiber dry, corners dried so nothing molds.

The training is the quality control — you can't inspect your way to a standard you never wrote down.

Put the company on a documented operating system

True North Services LLC · three-form workflow

Every lead runs one path, and no distribution is finalized until the paperwork clears. Three mandatory forms feed a single master sheet, so the company stops relying on memory, text messages, and informal verbal agreements.

  • lead
  • Form 1 quote scheduling
  • appointment
  • Form 2 result & pricing
  • approval → job
  • Form 3 end-of-job
  • invoice → paid
What each form captures
  • Form 1 — quote scheduling & assignmentClient details, property address, service type, lead source, preferred contact, requested time, assigned manager.
  • Form 2 — quote result & pricingFinal scope, before photos, quoted price, estimated labor and materials, proposed start date, payment terms, close probability, approval status.
  • Form 3 — end-of-job reportActual labor hours, materials used, after photos, property condition, satisfaction confirmation, damage issues, upsell opportunities, equipment problems.
  • The master sheetLeads, quotes, closed jobs, scheduling, payments, applicants, referral partners, manager performance, equipment, and a weekly scorecard — reviewed on a weekly ops call.

No form, no distribution. The paperwork is the control.

Incorporated True North Services LLC

Founder & owner · North Shore, Illinois

Took a summer cleaning business and made it a company: the LLC filing, an operations and risk framework, a written priority roadmap, and a manager-contractor model in which managers are independent contractors who own their crews, their scheduling, and their execution risk. Team of four-plus, with an operations lead running weekly ops calls.

The economics and the guardrails
  • Revenue splitOf gross: 20% company (brand, systems, overhead, growth), 5% backend (systems, data, forms, admin), 5% lead fee to whoever produced the job, 70% manager — who covers labor, materials, and margin risk.
  • Pricing floorsA $100 minimum job size, a 30%+ manager margin target, and a pricing-floor check before any quote goes out. Underpriced jobs get reviewed before distribution.
  • Liability structureJobs over $500 require a signed client agreement before work begins; managers sign a scope-and-indemnification agreement; before/after photos are required on property-sensitive jobs; card fees are passed through at a flat rate.
  • Service boundary matrixDirect services — power washing, window washing, weeding, mulching, bush trimming, staining, pavers, gravel paths, seasonal cleanups. Referral-only — roofing, plumbing, electrical, large tree work, licensed trades. Anything outside the list is approval-only.
  • CashBank transfer first, cash second, card third. Revenue lands in the LLC account; distributions run biweekly once documentation, disputes, and refunds are settled.

From hustle to institution — docs, training, and structure that run without you in the room.
